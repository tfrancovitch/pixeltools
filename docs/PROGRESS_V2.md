- 2026-11-22 **C1 done** (Python only). New `viewcheck.py` replaces the v1 line test: works on the original picture (no grid needed), weighs each spot by how coherent its edge direction is over a wider window (so foliage stops hiding straight lines), looks at the narrow classic isometric slopes (23-34 degrees, both rising and falling) against horizontal, and answers "unclear" when edge directions are spread evenly (round shapes). Blur is sized to the pixel for crisp pixel art (flatness 0.92 or more) and for native-size images. Batch 1, non-character pictures: **15 of 15 right** (6 isometric, 9 orthographic, none unclear); gate met: all 6 clear isometric found, no orthographic called isometric. Closest margin: church 0090, weaker diagonal 0.07 vs cut-off 0.045 (straight-on pictures 0.02 and below). Lessons recorded: (1) the first thresholds, tuned only on soft Midjourney images, falsely called drawn ellipses and a 2:1-gabled front house isometric, which would have misread round objects (barrels, pots, apples) in the object batch; the entropy guard and the narrow slope band fixed both; (2) staircase edges in crisp pixel art need a pixel-sized blur. 43 tests pass (7 new, `test_view.py`). Provisional until batch 2 and the object batch. Next: slice D1 (find ground shadows), or the browser port.
- 2026-11-22 **D1 done** (Python only). New `shadow.py` finds the ground shadow under a lone sprite: the ground colour times one factor (0.45-0.92) in all three channels, in a flat patch lying on open ground at the subject's base, touching the subject, wider than tall. Reports side (dx) and darkness for slice D2. Honest limits: white or near-white grounds answer "can't tell" (pale grey shadow vs pale grey parts of the sprite), shadows darker than 45% of the ground are treated as subject (so 0034's black shadow on a dark floor is missed), scenes and sprites on full painted backgrounds are not examined. Batch 1: 7 of 10 labelled shadows found (misses 0034, 0061, 0091), 0 false alarms among 16 shadow-free pictures on coloured grounds, 64 "can't tell" (white grounds). Drawn tests (36: four grounds x three shadow shapes x three darknesses): overlap (IoU) mean 0.91, worst 0.87; recall mean 0.93, precision 0.97+. Gate "90% overlap" met on average, not on every case: recorded rather than loosened. Labels corrected after a closer look: 0058 none (was contact), 0034 cast (was none). Lessons: the first loose rule ("a darker copy of the ground") also matched grey clothing on white, navy trousers on blue ground, boots and blurred blends inside the sprite; the fixes were ground contact (a shadow's edge meets open ground), deciding what is shadow before deciding what is subject, a flat-on-the-ground shape test and a darkness floor. Next: D2 (contact or cast, and the keep / remove-cast / remove-all setting with pixel-exact removal), or the browser port.

- 2026-11-22 **C2 ended: the stop rule applied.** Gate: at least 5 of the 6 clear diagonal characters found, at most 3 of the 43 clear cardinal ones called diagonal, within two tuning passes. Pass 1: distributions of five geometric clues (silhouette symmetry, feet apart, feet level, torso-over-legs shift, head-off-torso) for both groups: heavy overlap. Pass 2: exhaustive search over single clues and pairs (1,042 candidate rules, `eval/facing_probe.py`): best 4 of 6 with 3 false calls, or 5 of 6 with 7; **no rule meets the gate**, and with that many rules against six positives even the best are expected by chance. Shipped instead: `facing.py` measures the clues, `Result.facing` is "unclear" for characters and "n/a" otherwise, the clues go into `report.csv` (`facing_clues`), characters are "can't tell" when a facing is wanted (reason: "the tool cannot judge facing yet"), and the C3 decision table is ready for a future rule. "Mixed" facing was not attempted. What would change this: many more labelled characters (labelling game L1-L3) or a face and pose reader (a model, outside the "no AI" rule; Thomas's call). 78 tests pass (7 new in `test_facing.py`).
